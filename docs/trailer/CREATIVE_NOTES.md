# Creative decisions and research

This film invites the viewer to risk a throw, outwit a defender and get the
slipper home. That is the emotional promise. Rules and powers support it.

## Hook and visual storytelling

Derek Lieu's [cold-open essay](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2018/9/29/the-cold-open)
argues for a short, understandable action or question before background context.
Our application: show the ordinary can, Rago's commitment and contact before the
stadium reveal. No studio-logo preamble, history lecture or unexplained spectacle.
The first physical result arrives at about 4.47 seconds. The open question then
changes from whether he can hit it to whether he can retrieve his slipper.

Lieu's [goal-versus-task essay](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/2/25/show-the-dream-not-the-job)
distinguishes the player's aspiration from a list of mechanical chores. Our
application: make retrieval a contest of nerve and timing. Show both real modes
through consequences, rather than stopping for thirteen ability descriptions.
Do not describe an invented victory or outcome the selected footage cannot prove.

## Pacing and attention

The [rising-action essay](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2018/9/16/the-trailer-line-of-rising-action)
provides a useful model for changing energy across an edit. Our application is
specific to the owner's fixed blocks: immediate contact, a larger reveal, a
held decision, playable exchanges, then the authored escape. The street payoff
reduces the scale before the title. Cut lengths shorten in the late gameplay
sequence, while decisive contacts receive enough time to be understood.

Every camera has a subject and resolving state. Wide shots establish the can,
runner, defender and exit; close shots earn their place through a throw or grip.
The geometry checks support hand contact and framing. They do not certify acting
or emotional impact. Background-only overnight review uses ordered frame
sequences, including dense contact/boundary samples. Owner playback remains the
final creative judgment.

## Sound, identity and delivery

All sound belongs to the team. Leave room for the first can contact, the stadium
opening, the grip and the final title. The cue sheet names opportunities, not a
requirement for a sound at every edit. One recognizable musical idea can grow
with the world and return at human scale. Avoid keeping every layer at maximum
intensity for two minutes.

Use actual TUMP characters, court art, logo and Paalalabas typography. Preserve
the readable blocky silhouettes and flat faces. The master is 16:9/1080p30 with
essential action inside the frame and no dependence on narration. Keep that full
composition for showcase screens, web embeds and social reposts. A narrow crop
needs its own composition review; automatic cropping would remove opponents and
weaken the gameplay evidence.

The source timeline, selected media, native event records, replay archives and
frame-based sync handoff preserve decisions for another editor. These references
informed our treatment; their footage, music and scripts are not reused.
