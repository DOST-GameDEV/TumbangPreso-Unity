## 1. Teardown of v2

| Problem | Where | Rule it broke |
|---|---|---|
| No protagonist, no want, no change | The whole cut was unrelated vignettes | A story needs someone who wants something, an obstacle and a turn |
| Words did the storytelling | Five title cards in 40 s | Show, don't tell; cards only for what pictures cannot say |
| No real gameplay | Zero frames of play | A game trailer must show the game, and teach it while it excites |
| Borrowed footage | Train, jeepney, laughing shots from the map films, HUD still on | Everything on screen is made for this trailer |
| Effect before cause | Tsinelas into the stadium, then a crane out, then the can is hit | Cause and effect must touch: the hit lands on the cut, the reveal is the payoff after it |
| Energy random | Loud stadium at 0:07, then quiet cards, quiet archive, quiet old man, cards again | Energy must climb in steps with deliberate valleys (stopdowns) |
| No motif | Shots shared nothing | Repetition with variation is what makes a montage feel like one story |

## 2. The story

**Logline.** Totoy is always the taya. His lolo gives him the tsinelas he threw in the very
first Pandaigdig, and with it the history of how a street game became the world's game.

**Theme.** It began on an ordinary street, and it is handed down. Hand to hand, throw to throw.

**Characters.**

| Who | Model | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Totoy | `totoy` (Classic) | Our kid. Wants to knock the can down for once |
| Lolo Kanor | `mang_kanor` | Played in Pandaigdig 1974. Gives the tsinelas |
| Young Kanor, 1974 | `kuya_boy` | The first famous throw |
| Tatay as a boy, 1996 | `tikboy` | Copies Lolo's throw on the same street |
| The street kids | `tikboy`, `bebang`, `jun_jun` | Real bots in the real match |
| The pro | Rago (`sean`) | The dream: the same throw in the Philippine Arena |

**The motif: one throw, four generations.** Kanor 1974, Tatay 1996, Totoy now, Rago in the
Arena all throw with the same pose (`Pose.Charge` then `Contact` then `Follow`). The montage cuts
between them on the same frame of the motion. That single repeated action is the spine.

**The object.** The tsinelas. Introduced (Lolo's bag), explained (the 1974 sole), used (Totoy's throw).

**Sound motifs.** The kid's "Taya!" opens it. The tin CLANG on the street returns, huge and layered,
as the Arena hit: a sonic match cut.

## 4. Rules applied, checked per shot

- **Hook in 5 s:** real, funny gameplay by 0:01 with a laugh by 0:07.
- **Teach the game without cards:** B shows the taya guarding, the throw, the knock-down and the
  retrieval scramble; H shows the first-person aim. All real gameplay.
- **Cause touches effect:** the tsinelas leaves the lens and hits the can in the same breath (K);
  the reveal (L) comes after the hit, never before.
- **Cut on action and match cut:** sole to 1974 (D to E); same throw pose across E, F, J, L; tsinelas
  into lens and out of lens (J to K); street CLANG to Arena CLANG.
- **One idea per shot**, the subject never blocked, no camera move ending inside a body.
- **Show, don't tell:** one title card in 43 s. The TV's own broadcast graphic says PANDAIGDIG.
- **Aspect ratio:** 4:3 for the street and the past; widens to 2.39 only on the Arena hit.
- **Nothing borrowed.** No map-film footage. No HUD.
- **Acting:** anticipation, peak, overshoot and settle on every action; nobody frozen; crowds staggered.
