## Sources actually inspected

1. **Sepak U, developer description.** [Good Knight Collective's page](https://teamgoodknight.itch.io/sepak-u) describes a cultural sport expanded through fighting-game movement, distinctive characters and strong audiovisual presentation. It explicitly connects its presentation to sports, film and anime. This is a design reference, not evidence that TUMP should adopt its mechanics or planned roster size.
2. **Sepak U gameplay frames.** [Official gameplay trailer, distributed by GameTrailers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZMitelrRxs), sampled in the browser around 0:09, 0:12 and 0:18. At 0:09, FIRST HIT, score/type text, the bright ball, a violet impact shape, foot dust and trajectory residue share the play. At 0:12 the large text has cleared; the bright ball, broad pale trail and grounded actors carry the rally. At 0:18, a stable court-wide composition preserves both sides. The supplied photo shows the same presentation family. These are visual observations from sampled frames, not frame-accurate measurements or an audio review. The private photo was not imported into game assets.
3. **Blue Lock: Rivals, creator footage.** [Chrollo's KAISER ANNOUNCEMENT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bKkty9gWzOc), source channel verified in the player, includes commentary as well as gameplay. The sampled ending around 6:11 uses a trophy-centered group pose; gameplay at about 9:17 shows a saturated character/arena treatment, persistent match readout, named players and a separate lower ability strip. Do not mistake the video's commentary edits for gameplay UI or assume an announcement is representative of every current match.
4. **Blue Lock: Rivals, published game description.** [The Roblox listing](https://www.roblox.com/games/18668065416/Blue-Lock-Rivals) describes character classes, unique abilities and awakening. It identifies itself as a fan-made experience. We use the character-performance idea, not its branding, monetization or an assumption that its mechanics fit TUMP.
5. **Blue Lock reference boundary.** [The official anime character/video site](https://bluelock-anime-en.com/) supplies character-specific sporting identities; [Blaze Battle's official site](https://bluelock-blaze.jp/) is a separate game and currently reports its service ended on 31 March 2026. These are not interchangeable sources. No current Blaze Battle playtest was performed, and no specific cutscene behavior is asserted from its promotional site alone.
6. **Lethal League Blaze.** [Team Reptile's press kit](https://team-reptile.com/press/sheet.php?p=Lethal_League_Blaze) explicitly describes a limited-animation approach serving extreme-speed projectile play. The transferable proposition is strong poses and accents rather than making every body transition equally smooth. No target timing was measured from this source.
7. **Omega Strikers, developer interview.** [Odyssey's interview hosted by Unreal Engine](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/odyssey-interactive-a-new-studio-composed-of-former-riot-games-devs-talks-about-creating-multiplatform-game-omega-strikers) discusses approachable objective play, cohesive knockback interactions and a hopeful, energetic identity developed around how a character plays. It supports keeping TUMP's physical sport and character identity connected.
8. **Riot's VFX discipline.** [Visual Effects](https://www.riotgames.com/en/artedu/visual-effects) connects effect design with action/state communication and recognizable sources. TUMP adaptation: each simultaneous layer gets a clear purpose and owner; density alone is not a defect.
9. **Importance and silhouettes.** [Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/clarity-in-league/) discusses importance hierarchy, recognizable silhouettes and matching effect meaning to mechanics. TUMP adaptation: make decisive events more expressive while keeping the can, possession, facing and legal danger legible.
10. **Rendering and lower settings.** [VALORANT Shaders and Gameplay Clarity](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity) explains separating render priorities and preserving gameplay-relevant VFX when scaling cosmetic detail. TUMP adaptation: Low can reduce decorative debris and secondary glow while keeping truthful boundaries, motion direction and state distinctions.

## What changes in TUMP's direction

The previous plan correctly removed contradictory ordinary feedback and protected control. It did not yet specify enough coordinated spectacle, personality, animation continuity or audiovisual rhythm. The current private six-hero scenes establish working bodies, effects and sound capture; they are not finished cinematic direction. A valid renderer and a clean frame do not establish a memorable performance.

The revised plan makes the whole exchange the authoring unit. Several layers may coexist: body/FPP action, object motion, directional VFX, material sound, personal confirmation, world reaction, a readable score accent and an earned broadcast graphic. Their phase, composition and purpose must agree. We will tune conflicting emphasis instead of applying a blanket rule to reduce everything.

The Sepak U frame is especially useful because its emphatic typography does not replace the sporting action. TUMP can use bold earned graphics, but a can knockdown begins its retrieval/chase instead of ending play. The treatment must carry attention into that next action. A spectator can receive a larger editorial composition than an attacker aiming at the can, while both see the same accepted outcome.

## Limits of this research

No source game was installed, no paid service was used, and no source assets were copied. The Sepak U developer website timed out, so its developer itch page and published footage were used. Browser footage was sampled visually; no claim is made to have heard or measured its mix. The linked developer principles inform our choices; the detailed TUMP sequences, timings and task order are proposals to validate in this repository. Community search results were used only to locate original footage, not as technical authority.
