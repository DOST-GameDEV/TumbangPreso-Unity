## Experience before a feature list

Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek distinguish implemented mechanics, behaviour emerging
in play, and the emotional experience players seek. Their framework treats fun as
multiple possible experiences rather than one universal formula.
[MDA, original paper,2004](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf).

TUMP application (design inference): judge changes by the actual retrieval contest:
can a player read an opportunity, choose a route/action, execute it, understand the
outcome and want another attempt? Intended qualities are playful rivalry, suspense,
agency, mastery and satisfying physical expression. More assets, successful tests
or constantly moving bots are not substitutes for those experiences.

Questions to answer before changing a subsystem:

- What player intent is it serving? Is the goal/opportunity understandable?
- Are there useful choices with legible costs and counterplay?
- Does the input produce a timely, consistent response?
- Can player and spectator connect action, contact and consequence?
- Does the pacing alternate anticipation, commitment, outcome and recovery?
- Is friction intentional challenge, or confusion/stalling/broken behaviour?
- Does the change help this game's identity rather than merely add complexity?

## Appealing stylisation is authored selection

Valve describes recognisable silhouettes, environment-specific shape/material
language, controlled colour accents and painterly surface detail that avoids
excessive visual noise. Asset construction and shading were developed together to
support recognition during play.
[Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress2,2007, sections4.1-4.3](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf).

TUMP application (design inference): separate limbs from the torso in readable
poses; give each map/structure a clear construction identity; use recesses, trim,
material-specific marks and selected small detail around a strong large shape.
A quiet surface can be pleasing when the whole asset has hierarchy. Random grime,
universal grain and realism for its own sake are not the answer to blandness.
Coverage numbers or a material-family label do not establish aesthetic quality.

## Motion, art and feedback need a coherent intention

Tango's developers describe a deliberately coherent illustrated style and extensive
coordination of animation, action timing, sound and environment motion. They kept
movement exceptions where rhythm constraints felt restrictive and prioritised stable
performance while building the look.
[Hi-Fi RUSH developer interview,2023](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/hi-fi-rush-was-inspired-by-shaun-of-the-dead-and-futurama).

TUMP application (design inference): align preparation/release/contact/recovery and
sound with real game events; retain responsive controls and actual authoritative
outcomes. Borrow clarity and timing discipline, not that game's rhythm rules or
entire2Dstyle. Inspect moving silhouettes and hand/slipper separation at normal
speed. A dramatic frozen pose cannot prove that a walk or pektus looks natural.

## Working acceptance criteria to refine during full research

Play: readable goals, useful decisions, fair counterplay, responsive controls,
consistent consequences, appropriate challenge and reasons to re-engage.
Visuals: coherent proportions/palette, purposeful focal hierarchy, distinct material
and construction cues, depth/attachment, restrained detail frequency and readable
actors/objective across actual cameras. Realism is not a target.
Motion: intention, balance/weight, rhythm, clear arcs and silhouettes, contact,
follow-through, continuity and character-specific fit. Preserve the simple rig.
Satisfaction: audiovisual emphasis at the right event, contrast between quiet and
peak, meaningful success/failure distinction, and an outcome the player caused.

These are proposed evaluation questions, not proof that a particular implementation
will be fun or visually accepted. Apply them to one concrete aspect at a time and
compare the real result, with bounded verification and honest remaining limits.
