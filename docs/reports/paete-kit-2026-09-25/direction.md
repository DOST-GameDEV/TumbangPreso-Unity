## 0. Who he is on screen, in one paragraph

Paete is the slowest, calmest thing on the court, and every move he makes should feel like **a tree
deciding to act**. Weight first: the root sinks, the trunk bends, the arms come last (the load), and
then everything is released at once like a branch let go (the snap). He never flails. The world
answers him with GROWTH: things come up out of the ground, grow along it, wrap round what they
catch, and at the end wither back into it. His light is only three things (his eyes, his palms and
the sentry's core), all the same pale yellow-green, so a glance at the screen tells you which of his
things is live.

The references, and exactly what each one gives him:

| Reference | What we take | What we do NOT take |
|---|---|---|
| Groot, Marvel Rivals: Strangling Prison, Vine Strike, walls | the glowing seed thrown, the burst into a bright core with radial spiked vines, bodies held UPRIGHT round it; vines as dark lines, not glowing ones; walls erupting as a burst of trunks | the realistic bark detail and scale; his face acting |
| Kinich, Genshin (Dendro grapple) | the anchor mark, the grapple that always travels, the curved ribbon of the path | the bright particle line (his vine is solid, dark) |
| Pathfinder, Apex; Spider-Man | a short reel with a lift arc, arms locked on the anchor, legs trailing | a real pendulum (too long for a 14 m box) |
| Zyra, League; Yaoyao, Genshin; Maokai | a seed that POPS into a plant, the head-back snap-forward recoil of a plant that shoots, a summon that fights for you | plants that look like creatures |
| Scorpion, Mortal Kombat | catch, a HOLD beat of tension, then the yank | the violence |
| Dead by Daylight | the struggle loop and a bar that fills on the victim's own screen | the horror framing |
| Zarya, Overwatch | the radius read of a pull to one point | floating bodies (ours stand, feet on the court) |

## 2. Art rules for every effect (so they are one family)

1. **Solid, not glowing.** Vines, bark, thorns and leaves are toon-lit geometry in his own palette.
   Only the eyes, the palms and the core glow.
2. **Things GROW, they do not scale.** A vine grows along its own path from its root; a thorn grows
   from its base. Scaling a finished shape from zero is allowed only for pop-ups (the seedling, the
   trunk erupting), and then with an overshoot (`GrowthVfx.Pop`).
3. **Everything ends by withering**: curl, darken, drop leaves, sink. Never fade a solid by alpha.
4. **Every catch has a hold beat** before the pull.
5. **Blocky, cute, readable on Low**: square thorns, stepped trunks, six-sided leaves. No realistic
   bark noise. The can, slippers and players stay readable through every one of these.
6. **Hand-typed, part by part** (the owner's rule): every part its own numbers, never a loop stamping
   one shape round a circle with the same size.

### 5.1 The idea: Makiling's forest is his family

Every tree he makes is an echo of Paete himself, so a glance ties it to him: a woven wooden body,
moss capping the TOP faces (where it would grow), square-cut branches with right-angle elbows and
small tines like his antlers, clusters of big six-sided leaves at the ends, and the pale yellow-green
light in exactly the places HE has it. The sentry is the mountain's guardian: it has his face. The
seedling is a child: a pod that behaves like a head, no face. The thorn construct is a clenched fist
of roots.

### 5.2 THE SENTRY (YAKAP NG MAKILING), 4.3 m

**Model, part by part.**
- *Foot:* six buttress roots gripping the road, each its own reach, girth and bend, with a knuckle
  where it dives into the road and a moss cap on top. They belong to the ground, not the trunk, so
  the trunk can turn above them.
- *Trunk, the weave (the owner's reference):* a rope of about a dozen cords, low poly (five sides,
  so it stays in the blocky cast), in three plies of four that twist round each other while each ply
  twists its own cords the other way, the way a real rope is laid. Cords are each their own girth and
  bark shade, so the weave shows cord by cord; they part at the top into the crown branches and at
  the foot into the roots, so the weave is the SAME wood as both. Two or three fine dark vines run
  IN THE GROOVES between plies, never round the outside.
- *Face,* at 2.0 to 2.6 m, looking DOWN on the prisoners (their heads are at 1.9 m): a mask of carved
  planks bound onto the front of the weave; two heavy brow planks slanting in (his calm, deep-set
  look); two sunken sockets with the eye light at the back; a nose ridge; a mouth cut in ink; his
  spiral on the forehead plank. The eyelids and brows are their own nodes.
- *Vines with a story,* none an even spiral: one climbs out of a buttress root up a groove to the
  shoulder; one loops over a crown branch and hangs in a swag; one crosses the chest under the face
  like his own torso vine. Leaves only at their tips and a few joints.
- *Moss:* caps on top faces only (root knuckles, the shoulders where the cords part, crown elbows).
- *Crown:* five square-cut branches with right-angle elbows and a tine or two each (his antlers,

- *Core:* the seed of light held in the crown (procedural, kept). The ONLY light besides the eyes.

### 5.3 THE SEEDLING (PUNLANG TSINELAS), 0.9 m

**Model.** A small three-cord twisted stem (the sentry's weave, as a child), a moss collar; two big
leaves low on the stem that act as its ARMS; four roots gripping; the POD is its head: a bud of five
petals round the growing wooden slipper, on a short neck so it can nod and aim.

**Animation.**
- Pop (0 to 0.45 s): pushes up out of the soil, overshoot, squash, the arm-leaves flick open last.
- Alive: a slow sway, the pod bobbing a little behind the stem (follow-through); the arm-leaves breathe.
- Ripening: the petals part as the slipper grows; when it is ready they are wide and the pod gives
  a small proud bounce. NEW `sfx_paete_sprout_ready`, a soft pod pop, so the owner hears a shot is
  ready without looking.
- Fire: anticipation (stem compresses, pod pulls back 0.12 s, arm-leaves flare), the SNAP forward
  past rest, then a wobble to rest.
- Loosening (15 s on): leaves dry toward straw, the stem sags, roots lift, a breathing ring of loose
  soil (kept).
- Pulled: roots rip up, it is flung spinning toward the puller (kept).

### 5.7 How the builder is written (owner: "manually do each part of that builder dont js auto generate looping shit")

The first v2 draft still drew the weave from one helix formula looped over a table of cords. Replaced:
every cord of the weave is its own list of hand-typed control points (height, angle round the trunk,
distance out, girth), with the over-and-under crossings placed by hand; every root, branch, tine,
leaf, thorn, petal and moss patch is typed as its own points. Interpolation between typed points (a
spline) is the only arithmetic between them.

### 5.8 How the prisoners are held (owner: "characters should look tied to the tree", "thoroughly think abt the way they should be tied", "dont use vines use woven tree branches")

The owner sent a picture of someone tied to a trunk with vines, arms bound overhead, as a pointer to
the IDEA ("so u know what i mean"), not to copy. What it asks for: the prisoners must read as HELD BY
THE TREE, not as players who happen to stand near it. What we do not take from it: arms bound (a
rooted player can still throw and cast, the owner's own rule: *"THEY CAN STILL THROW AND USE SKILLS
WHILE STUCK BTW theyre js rooted"*), anything over the mouth or face, and the helpless framing. TUMP
is a playful street sport.

The ultimate is YAKAP, the embrace, so the tree HUGS them:

1. **The embrace limb.** For each prisoner a woven limb (three bark cords laid round each other, the
   trunk's own weave in miniature) leaves the weave at 1.5 to 1.8 m on the side facing them, arcs out
   and down, and wraps their WAIST in a band of about one and a half turns, the cords parting into
   separate strands as they wrap, a twig and a leaf or two at the band's end. Arms and head stay free.
   The limb is taut (a slight curve, never slack) and creaks and sways a little.
2. **The shin roots.** Woven root-branches climb their shins from the road (replacing the green vine
   coil), bark in three shades, a knot at the knee, a leaf at a tip. They grow up over 0.6 s.
3. **Upright**, feet planted, facing the tree loosely. When they struggle (holding Interact) the band
   shakes, the limb flexes and the shin branches creak (`rooted-struggle` already carries the body).
4. **Breaking out** (5.6): the band cracks into bark chunks and the limb whips back into the trunk; the
   shin branches split and fall.
5. **Only wood, moss and leaves.** No green vines in the binding, and the tree's own vines become thin
   woven branches with leaves on them.
