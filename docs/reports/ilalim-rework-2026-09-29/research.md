# ILALIM-1.1 research: Taft Avenue at Padre Faura, under LRT-1 (2026-09-29)

This is the evidence behind the proposal in [ILALIM_REWORK_GUIDE § 0](../../ILALIM_REWORK_GUIDE.md).
The owner asked for "the real place under the LRT-2 guideway, preferrably around the UP Manila
Padre Faura area (cuz we wanna see our school's Rizal Hall in the game)".

## 1 · A correction first: this is LRT-1 on Taft, not LRT-2

The elevated line past UP Manila is **LRT-1**, opened in 1984 and built over **Taft Avenue**.
LRT-2 runs along Aurora Boulevard and past Gilmore, which is the setting the old design document
describes ("the LRT Gilmore strip"). The nearest LRT-1 stations are **United Nations** (north) and
**Pedro Gil** (south, at the Pedro Gil corner). There is no station at Padre Faura itself.
Sources: [Taft Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taft_Avenue),
[Pedro Gil station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Gil_station).

## 2 · The layout, measured from OpenStreetMap

The footprints come from one Overpass query centred on (14.5785, 120.9855), with a 350 m
radius, fetched 2026-09-29. The query returned named buildings and amenities, the named
roads, the LRT-1 ways and the stations. The results were projected to local metres, with x
east and y north. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
The plan is drawn in [place-plan-v1.png](place-plan-v1.png) (source: `place-plan-v1.svg`).

- **Taft Avenue** runs north-north-west to south-south-east. It is a divided avenue: two one-way
  carriageways of 3 to 4 lanes each. The LRT-1 viaduct follows it for the whole block.
- **Padre Faura Street** is one-way westbound, with 3 lanes. It meets Taft from the west at about
  (60, 150) and continues east of Taft with an offset.
- **Rizal Hall** is at x -98..-29, y 113..188. It stands on the NORTH side of Padre Faura,
  about 100 m west of Taft, and faces south onto the street. The PGH frontage is across the
  street. The Gat Andres Bonifacio Building is behind it.
- **The Taft/Padre Faura corners:**

  | Corner | What stands there |
  |---|---|
  | North-west | The Supreme Court complex. Its white columned facades line Padre Faura toward Rizal Hall. |
  | North-east | Manila Science High School, 10 storeys. |
  | South-west | Philippine General Hospital: the Out-Patient Department and the Nurses Home. |
  | South-east | A dense commercial strip. |

- **The east side of Taft**, south of Padre Faura, is continuous small commercial buildings of 2
  to 6 storeys: food, clinics, offices and mid-rise residences. The 19-storey **Astral Tower**
  has cream and salmon horizontal bands and is the landmark on the skyline.
- **The west side of Taft** is the UP Manila and PGH campus: 14 hectares of heritage buildings by
  William E. Parsons, with red roofs, among trees.

Sources for the institutions and their history:

- [CAS history](https://cas.upm.edu.ph/~pjperez/history.html)

## 3 · Rizal Hall, from photographs

The building was designed by William Parsons, the PGH architect, and completed in 1921. The
quadrangle and the east wing were finished by 1926. Work continued until 1932.

What the photographs show:
- **Walls:** three storeys in ivory stucco.
- **Portico:** tall round **Ionic columns** carry an entablature with a **dentil cornice** and
  round rosette medallions.
- **Roof:** hipped, in red-brown, with deep eaves and exposed rafters.
- **Windows and balconies:** dark steel-sash windows in deep reveals, and iron balcony railings
  on the side bays.
- **Name:** **"RIZAL HALL" in maroon serif letters** over a moulded entrance frame.
- **Everyday details:** window aircon units and split-type condensers stuck on the facade and
  on the cornice. They are a very Manila detail.
- **Trees and approach:** palms and broadleaf trees in front, and front steps that face the
  Oblation statue.

## 4 · The street under the viaduct, from photographs

- **The LRT-1 viaduct:**
  - The deck is a **narrow grey concrete box** with a projecting parapet lip and a flat, dark
    soffit, marked by regular panel joints.
  - It stands on **single square piers**.
  - Catenary masts stand on top of the deck.
  - At a station, the platform level carries a pitched metal roof on posts.
- **The trains** are **yellow and blue**, and run as short multi-car sets.
- **Taft, looking across at Padre Faura:**
  - tangled overhead cables on leaning poles;
  - jeepneys, UV Express vans, buses and motorcycles;
  - striped vendor umbrellas on the kerb;
  - painted steel crowd barriers;
  - green street-name blades, black ONE WAY signs, and traffic lights on mast arms.
- **Padre Faura, looking east toward Taft:**
  - white columned heritage facades behind ornamental white fences;
  - shade trees along both kerbs;
  - yellow-painted steel railings;
  - parked vans and pickups;
  - students walking in the road;
  - the LRT viaduct crossing the end of the street, with Astral Tower rising behind it.
- **The campus from above:** a sea of **red roofs** among dense trees, beside the grey viaduct
  line, with the traffic-choked Taft beside it.
- **Barangay signage near Pedro Gil:** big hand-painted shaped boards in yellow with red
  lettering. They name the chairperson and the kagawads.
