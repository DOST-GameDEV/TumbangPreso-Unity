## The rules

1. **The show ends once.** A reveal plays one time, lands, and never restarts. Replaying it behind
   a menu is what makes a home screen tiring. Marvel Rivals' whole action lasts about 10 s.
2. **Land on the composition the player will live with.** The last shot of the reveal IS the idle
   frame, chosen first and worked backward from. No cut or fade between them.
3. **Hold still after landing.** After the landing there are no cuts and only slow camera drift.
   Motion comes from small things: breath, a fidget, weather, traffic.
4. **Bring the UI up at the landing, not before.** The eye should get the landing first. In TUMP
   the hub's UI is already up, so the reveal must read around it from frame one (rule 5).
5. **Compose for the gap.** Gekko's subject always sits between the left list and right cards.
   Nothing busy goes under a button in any shot, not only in the idle.
6. **Build from small to whole.** Detail first, whole hero last. The full figure is the payoff, so
   the first shots should not spend it.
7. **One accent colour carries the calm.** Neon's wrist, the card edge. For Zack: narrow yellow
   current. Big effects are only for the single peak.
8. **Hold each shot long enough to name.** Reference shots run 2 to 3 s even in fast cuts. This
   also keeps the method's first standing rule: every beat breathes.
9. **Two characters beat one in the hold.** A second figure with an attitude gives the idle a
   relationship to look at for minutes.
10. **The idle must never show a seam.** Everything in it is periodic on the idle's own length.

## What this means for TUMP

Plain video is enough for this. Two clips per hero, a one-time film and a seamless idle whose first
frame is the film's last frame, with the idle started while that frame is held.
