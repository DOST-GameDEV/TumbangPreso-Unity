# Method, provenance and limits

## Playback finding

The connected Windows PC's installed local browser loaded the official YouTube videos and presented actual moving video. For Yoimiya, playback advanced from the opening architecture shot into character and combat frames. Accepted samples at 00:30, 00:40 and 00:50 had nonzero decoded dimensions; the denser 00:32 through 00:49 samples had matching requested and presented timestamps, ready state 4 and 854 by 480 video frames. This disproves the earlier inference that black cloud-browser playback meant local playback could not work.

Mualani later loaded at approximately 01:06 from the ordinary 01:05 timestamp link; Keqing loaded at approximately 00:39 from the ordinary 00:38 timestamp link. Their inspected dense samples also had matching requested/presented times and ready state 4. Some first attempts to seek into later YouTube segments failed with a player error or zero video dimensions. Those frames are rejected, not described as footage. Seeking beyond Keqing's roughly 59.34 second duration returned the end rather than the requested time; those samples are also rejected. Reloading a normal timestamp URL supplied the later valid frames. No access restriction was bypassed.

## What was observed

Footage was played muted to verify time advancement and then paused at specific times for frame inspection. The report's observations concern those inspected frames and coarse sequences. It does not claim uninterrupted viewing of every trailer, fine frame-by-frame contact timing, or listening to music, voice, foley or effects. Official agent-page tabs were inspected to identify each named ability's actual video source; those official clips then played in the local browser. Clip-relative timestamps start at the clip's beginning, not a trailer timeline.

The Genshin references are promotional edits with camera cuts, graphic cards and potentially altered temporal presentation. A title card is not a gameplay HUD. Repeated bodies may be an edited motion illustration, not several gameplay actors. VALORANT first-person clips expose hand presentation and world response, but generally do not expose the opponent's camera or the caster's full body. Ability text supports source identification only; unshown gameplay is not promoted to direct observation.

Selected PNGs are unaltered browser player captures. Their native video resolution varied with adaptive playback, including 640 by 360, 854 by 480 and 1280 by 720; Jett's clip decoded at 1920 by 1080. Browser player controls can overlap the lower edge. Do not judge tiny surface detail, subtle silhouettes near the controls, or exact mesh contact from these captures. The JSON evidence preserves the underlying decoder resolution separately from screenshot dimensions.

## Limits that matter

Visible effect density cannot reveal the original engine's shader cost, draw calls, pooling, memory use or frame budget. Any performance recommendation here is a TUMP measurement requirement. A readable promotional shot cannot prove readable four-player retrieval. A frame without a shadow cannot prove a fair tell across TUMP graphics settings. A clean caster view cannot prove a credible Phaister victim illusion. Proposed beat sheets do not authorize mechanical delay, invulnerability, score, pickup, target acquisition or status changes.

No audio input was available to this review. Browser playback and the existence of an audio file are insufficient listening evidence. Material and event suggestions are unlistened production hypotheses. Timbre, fatigue, pleasantness, spatial clarity and mix superiority need an actual supported listening pass and timed in-game validation. Existing signal measurements elsewhere cannot close that gap.

## Self-critique

The initial elemental trio offers striking spectacle but weak evidence for opponent counterplay and grounded recovery. Later official first-person clips improve the local-to-world release analysis, yet still leave third-person biomechanics and victim perception unanswered. Sparse one-second or multi-second trailer samples miss fast transitions; therefore this report does not derive millisecond animation timings from them. Protected Paete/Phaister references should constrain future changes rather than create new polish work. The report sharpens implementation and review questions; it is not proof that a proposed treatment looks or sounds better than the authored game.

Only isolated research browser contexts were opened. Existing user tabs and profiles were not used. Research browsers closed in their own cleanup paths. No Unity job, account change, live profile change or runtime edit was performed.
