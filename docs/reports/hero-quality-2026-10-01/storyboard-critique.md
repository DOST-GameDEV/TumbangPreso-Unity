# Storyboard ideation and critique

Built-in image generation used as authorized ideation, not a game renderer or
proof of native quality. No paid API or model-version claim. Both original outputs
are retained in ArtSource/hero-quality-20261001/sean-storyboard-v1.png and v2.png.
Reference input: native September24 Sean contact sheet, not a generic voxel hero.

## Prompt direction

Six panels: inspect a stick, assemble an open five-stick parol between hands,
hold/read the completed form, compress through knees/hips, launch, return attention
to actual court/can/slipper. Preserve native proportions/face/outfit and simple
hands; no nose/eyebrows/new costume. Keep effects angular, warm, sparse and causal.
This is an exploration of a specific existing character motif, not its invention.

## First output review

Useful: parol remains open, Sean's face stays visible, sequence has a clear
small-to-large progression and panel4 loads more of the body than the old stills.
Rejected: invented articulated fingers, realistic smoky/bloom treatment, huge leap
above roofs, missing can/slipper in final panel. It is attractive illustration but
fails several game constraints. Do not port its anatomy, jump or particle density.

## One targeted correction

Second prompt explicitly corrected simple block hands, maximum modest leap,
visible blue can/dark slipper outside the landing ring and sparse angular effects,
while retaining six panels and the rest of the sequence.

Second output improves hand simplicity and final object visibility. It still has
important faults: panel5 is too high for the requested modest leap, the final shoe
is oversized by perspective, the can is already down before landing, and the star
persists in the hands through launch instead of visibly transferring its energy.
The painted lighting/material detail is not native-game fidelity. None of these
are accepted gameplay truths. No third cosmetic generation loop: native blocking
must now solve geometry, exact height, prop lifecycle and actual can state.

## Native direction taken, and not taken

Take the close hand-frame read, quiet completed-parol beat and full-body compression.
Do not take the floating props, invented fingers, stylised physics, existing-down
can or oversaturated court. Keep current registered rig and actual motor timing.
Draw three real camera keyframes using the authored model before adding effects.
The close-up must not hide the hand-frame joint; the wide shot must establish where
Sean really lands and where the real can is. A correct landing warning precedes
live contact. No image-generation artifact is shipped as a model, animation or HUD.

No sound was generated or evaluated by this image exercise. It cannot establish
animation timing, input response, collision, network agreement or final quality.
