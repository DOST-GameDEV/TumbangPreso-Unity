## Paete: character, rule and physical action are the same idea

His strongest quality is not the quantity of leaves. A patient forest guardian
brings things back. That identity governs his arms reeling his body, a plant that
grows an actual wooden slipper, thorns gathering the game's ammunition, and a
guardian holding people while they can still throw. Every prop belongs to the
street game's verbs. Bakya Bloom is locally specific without relying on a giant
folklore exposition panel. The plant is a character with a readable lifecycle:
seed, growth, readiness, recoil, vulnerability to uprooting and withering.

Source details worth transferring as principles:
- Planting and commanding are different actions with different body/FPP gestures.
  A command with no grown clog refuses honestly instead of performing a fake cast.
- The vine's arm connection sells cause and tension. A hold before the yank makes
  force legible. Other heroes should find their own material cause, not get vines.
- The ultimate ends in the state that real play adopts. It does not grow/catch
  everyone again after the introduction. Visual staging and authoritative outcome
  have different responsibilities, but must agree about who was caught.
- Captured people face outward and can continue throwing/casting. A control effect
  creates a new situation instead of reducing the victim to an inert spectator.
- Camera refinement addressed actual trunk/body occlusion and travel across the
  victim's route. A prettier storyboard did not count as fixing those frames.
- Endings express material change, rather than just deleting a prop at a timer.

Do not copy the guardian, carved face, bakya plant, pulling limbs, Makiling spirit,
root prison or his particular camera ride into other kits. Transfer: a strong
character verb, a physical causal chain, a small recognizable quirk, honest state
feedback and an ending that returns the scene to playable space.

## Phaister: a consistent causal language, not generic purple magic

The final kit's central explanation is what she does to the doll affects the
marked player. Reach, soul draw, wring/stab and victim feedback form a sequence
that observers can interpret. The hand-held curse prop and ultimate companion
have distinct jobs; the portal, puppeteer/control and autonomous doll are separate
entities. That separation makes a complicated scene comprehensible.

Source/history lessons:
- Earlier showman, black-hole and generic curse directions were explicitly retired.
  Their old plans are not permission to restore them. The active kit is Teleport,
  Curse: Drain, Curse: Hex and Voodoo Doll.
- An empty target refuses the reach rather than spending a long cooldown on a
  meaningless gesture. A broken reach has its own outcome. Failure is authored.
- The old soul connection looked like a physical rope; the owner wanted aura drawn
  into the doll. Correct material identity mattered more than a functioning line.
- Fear came from delayed revelation and being looked at, not from darkening the
  whole picture instantly. The final puppeteer plan separates the day draining,
  rise, seam, eye recognition, hands, descent and impact.
- Her smirk and the doll's slack/jerked locomotion distinguish two personalities.
  Simply shrinking Phaister into a companion was explicitly rejected.
- Real films exposed prop release before the hand released, unreadable first-person
  framing, silhouettes over the face, and a finale without actual targets. A green
  mechanics test was never accepted as proof that the performance worked.

Do not copy moth travel, stitched circles, pins, soul sucking, puppeteer hands,
rag-doll locomotion or her ominous eye. Transfer: a small material vocabulary,
clear roles for complex props, tension/release contrast, intentional acting,
viewpoint-specific framing and a believable handoff to the live actor.

## The transferable quality bar

For each new skill, write one sentence describing the character's intent and one
sentence describing the opponent's useful response. Then direct the six beats:
tell, release, travel, contact, linger, dissipate. For every moving part, specify
origin, anchor, direction, rhythm and ending. Give the first-person hands a real
job, including where the held slipper goes. Use quiet beats to make the important
beat powerful. Budget the field differently from the cinematic: the former must
leave can, shoes and threats readable. Tie sound to meaningful material/action
changes instead of putting a generic rising whoosh under every press.

Every skill plan needs the refused/missed/cancelled/interrupted/expired version,
not just the promotional perfect cast. Every ultimate needs a precise handoff:
what already happened during the scene, what the host resolves at release, and
what must not replay. A replay must show the accepted event, not improve its aim.
